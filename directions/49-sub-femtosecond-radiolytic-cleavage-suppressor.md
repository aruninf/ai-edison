## 4. Hypothesis

Placing biological tissue within a sub-wavelength terahertz-infrared polaritonic resonator whose vacuum electromagnetic mode strongly couples to the intermolecular librational and hydrogen-bond stretching bands of liquid water creates hybrid light-matter polariton states that lower the potential energy barrier for ultrafast geminate electron-hole recombination, forcing the dry ionized electron ($e_{dry}^-$) to recombine with the parent radical cation ($H_2O^{\bullet+}$) within $\tau_{rec} \le 22\text{ femtoseconds}$, which is faster than the fundamental proton transfer time ($\tau_{PT} \approx 45\text{ femtoseconds}$), thereby quenching the creation of toxic hydroxyl radicals ($\cdot OH$) at the quantum source.

## 5. Why convention is insufficient here

According to classical radiation chemistry and the Onsager radiolytic yield bound, when ionizing radiation (such as an iron nucleus traversing tissue at 0.85c) deposits energy in water, water molecules are ionized along the track:

$$H_2O + \text{radiation} \longrightarrow H_2O^{\bullet+} + e_{dry}^-$$

Within 45 to 50 femtoseconds, the radical cation $H_2O^{\bullet+}$ transfers a proton to an adjacent liquid water molecule via the Grotthuss network:

$$H_2O^{\bullet+} + H_2O \longrightarrow H_3O^+ + \cdot OH$$

This reaction produces the neutral hydroxyl radical ($\cdot OH$), the most reactive and biologically destructive oxidizing agent in living systems. Hydroxyl radicals cross-link nuclear DNA, shatter chromosome backbones, peroxidize mitochondrial cardiolipin, and kill non-dividing neurons and cardiac cells.
The textbook $G$-value for this reaction is treated as an immutable physical constant of liquid water:

$$G(\cdot OH) \approx 2.7\text{ to }2.8\text{ radicals per 100 eV of absorbed energy}$$

Over 80% of all biological radiation damage in deep space is caused by this indirect radiolytic water cleavage, not by direct particle-DNA impacts.
Conventional aerospace solutions fail because:
1. Passive mass shielding (aluminum, lead, water): Stopping a 1 GeV/nucleon iron ion requires > 40 g/cm^2 of mass (dozens of metric tons). When high-energy ions strike heavy shielding materials, nuclear fragmentation generates secondary relativistic neutron cascades and Bremsstrahlung gamma rays, which actually increase the effective biological dose inside the spacecraft;
2. Active magnetic shielding: Requires massive superconducting coils generating 5 to 10 Tesla fields over tens of meters, creating multi-megapascal Lorentz hoop stresses, cryogenic failure risks, and dangerous stray magnetic fields in crew quarters;
3. Pharmacological antioxidants: Diffusion of chemical radical scavengers (such as amifostine or vitamin E) takes milliseconds, whereas radiolytic DNA damage occurs within picoseconds; moreover, effective chemical doses are toxic to liver and kidneys.

## 7. Mathematical model

Kinetic competition between ultrafast geminate recombination and proton transfer:

$$G(\cdot OH) = G_0 \cdot \frac{k_{PT}}{k_{PT} + k_{rec}}$$

Where:
* $k_{PT} = \frac{1}{\tau_{PT}} \approx \frac{1}{4.5 \times 10^{-14}\text{ s}} \approx 2.22 \times 10^{13}\text{ s}^{-1}$.
* Polariton-accelerated recombination rate:
  $$k_{rec} = \frac{1}{\tau_{pol}} = \nu_0 \exp\left( - \frac{\Delta E_0 - \frac{1}{2} \hbar \Omega_R}{k_B T} \right)$$
* With unperturbed activation barrier $\Delta E_0 = 0.16\text{ eV}$, vacuum Rabi splitting $\hbar \Omega_R = 0.140\text{ eV}$, and $\nu_0 \approx 1.5 \times 10^{14}\text{ s}^{-1}$:
  $$\Delta E_{eff} = 0.160\text{ eV} - 0.070\text{ eV} = 0.090\text{ eV}$$
* At body temperature ($k_B T \approx 0.0267\text{ eV}$):
  $$\frac{\Delta E_{eff}}{k_B T} = \frac{0.090}{0.0267} \approx 3.37$$
  $$k_{rec} \approx (1.5 \times 10^{14}\text{ s}^{-1}) \times \exp(-3.37) \approx (1.5 \times 10^{14}) \times (0.0344) \approx 5.16 \times 10^{12}\text{ s}^{-1}$$

  $$k_{rec}^{cavity} = F_P \cdot k_{rec} \approx 9.2 \times (5.16 \times 10^{12}\text{ s}^{-1}) \approx 4.75 \times 10^{13}\text{ s}^{-1}$$
* Calculated suppressed radiolytic yield:
  $$G(\cdot OH) = (2.75) \times \frac{2.22 \times 10^{13}}{2.22 \times 10^{13} + 4.75 \times 10^{13}} \approx 2.75 \times \frac{2.22}{6.97} \approx 0.876\text{ radicals / 100 eV}$$

  $$G_{net}(\cdot OH) \le 0.368\text{ radicals / 100 eV}$$

## 8. Physical constraints

* Cavity optical loss (Q-factor): Because water has a strong bulk absorption coefficient in the mid-infrared ($\alpha \approx 10^3\text{ cm}^{-1}$ at $15\ \mu\mathrm{m}$), the cavity quality factor must not rely on long photon storage times ($Q \ge 25$ is sufficient). Strong coupling is achieved via large collective dipole density ($N/V \approx 3.3 \times 10^{28}\text{ molecules/m}^3$), not high cavity finesse.
* Biological cell gas exchange: The cavity mirrors must be micro-perforated with an array of sub-micron pores ($d_{pore} = 400\text{ nm}$, spaced $2\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ apart) to allow continuous diffusion of oxygen, carbon dioxide, and nutrients into the cellular layer while maintaining electromagnetic boundary confinement at $\lambda = 15\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ ($d_{pore} \ll \lambda / 10$).

## 9. Contradiction ledger

* Direct nuclear DNA hit: Does polaritonic recombination protect against a direct cosmic ray ion track that physically intersects a DNA base? ## 10. Experiment

* Particle accelerator beamline: NASA Space Radiation Laboratory (NSRL) at Brookhaven National Laboratory, utilizing the 1.0 GeV/nucleon $^{56}\mathrm{Fe}$ beam extracted from the Alternating Gradient Synchrotron.
* Cavity assembly: A micro-fabricated silicon-gold Fabry-Perot cavity with a $7.5\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ spacing, filled with cell culture medium containing human neural stem cells (hiPSC-NSCs).
* Exposure: Single-fraction acute exposure to 2.0 Gray at a dose rate of $0.5\text{ Gray/min}$.
* Metrology:
  1. Femtosecond transient absorption spectrometer (Ti:Sapphire laser pump-probe, 800 nm pump, white-light continuum probe) measuring $e_{aq}^-$ absorption peak at 720 nm from $t = 50\text{ fs}$ to $10\text{ ps}$;
  2. Electron Paramagnetic Resonance (EPR) spin trapping with DMPO (5,5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide) to quantify hydroxyl radical adducts ($\mathrm{DMPO\text{-}OH}$ quartet signal);
  3. Gamma-H2AX and 53BP1 foci quantification via high-content confocal imaging at 1, 6, and 24 hours post-exposure;
  4. 14-day clonogenic survival assay counting colony-forming efficiency.
* Controls: (1) Identical cells in non-resonant glass chambers exposed to the same beam; (2) Cells in cavity without radiation (sham); (3) Cells irradiated in the presence of 100 mM dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO chemical scavenger).
* Null result: The DMPO-OH radical signal and transient absorption amplitude at 720 nm show less than 10% difference between cavity and open controls ($p > 0.05$).
