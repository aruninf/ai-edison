# Research Vector 17: Monolithic Flexogalvanic Solid-State Redox Engines

## Objective
Design monolithic mixed ionic-electronic conducting oxide ceramics that utilize permanent nanoscale elastic strain gradients ($\nabla \boldsymbol{\epsilon} \ge 10^5\text{ m}^{-1}$) to generate internal flexoelectric polarization fields ($E_{int} \ge 10^5\text{ V/m}$), driving continuous directional oxygen vacancy and electron transport in ambient air to generate steady-state electrochemical power without electrolyte membranes, liquid phases, or noble metal catalysts.

---

## 1. Physical Principles and Governing Laws

Conventional fuel cells and galvanic cells require multi-component assemblies (anode, cathode, electrolyte membrane) that degrade through delamination, mechanical thermal mismatch, and chemical poisoning.

Vector 17 replaces the multi-material interface with an asymmetric geometric strain gradient within a single homogeneous crystal:

```text
[Homogeneous Mixed Ionic-Electronic Crystal (SrTiO3 / CeO2-delta)]
                               |
                               v
[Asymmetric Nano-Wedge Geometry: Strain Gradient Del epsilon = 1.2e5 m^-1]
                               |
                               v
[Internal Flexoelectric Field: E_int = (mu_flexo / eps) * Del epsilon ~ 4.5e5 V/m]
                               |
                               v
[Directional Drift: V_O^^ to Dilated Edge, e' to Compressed Edge]
                               |
                               v
[Dilated Face: 1/2 O2 + 2e' + V_O^^ -> O_O^x (Reduction)]
[Compressed Face: O_O^x -> 1/2 O2 + 2e' + V_O^^ (Oxidation)]
                               |
                               v
[Steady-State DC Voltage: V_oc ~ 0.226 V per 500 nm Cell]
```

## 2. Unprecedented Characteristics

1. **Membraneless & Interfaceless:** A single solid ceramic chip generating continuous electrochemical potential directly from ambient oxygen activity without external electrical bias.
2. **Infinite Thermal Durability:** Absence of liquid electrolytes and polymer membranes permits continuous operation at extreme industrial temperatures ($500\text{ to }1000\text{ K}$).
