# Vector 81: Static Alternating-Gradient Ponderomotive Electron-Ejection Insulator

**Status:** live
**Confidence:** 72%, basis: Static alternating-gradient (ponderomotive) repulsion in spatially periodic electric fields ($U_{\text{pond}} \propto |\nabla \phi_1|^2 / E_k$) is exact classical electrodynamics; first-half-period trajectory capture for electrons emitted directly inside high-field low-permittivity bands requires 3D particle-in-cell (PIC) verification.

## 1. Name

The Static Alternating-Gradient Ponderomotive Electron-Ejection Insulator (Trajectory-Engineered Vacuum Holdoff by Radial Ejection of Avalanche Electrons)

## 2. Fundamental problem

Surface microstructures and low-SEE coatings attempt to suppress vacuum surface flashover by modifying the wall material or limiting single-hop energy. However, as long as the vacuum electrostatic trajectory bends emitted electrons back toward the insulator wall (under positive surface charge $E_{\text{charge},x} \approx -1$ to $-3\ \mathrm{MV/m}$), electrons remain trapped in a surface-hugging hopping layer. To make a Secondary Electron Emission Avalanche (SEEA) physically unsustainable without external magnets or active power, the vacuum **trajectory itself** must be engineered so that emitted electrons are radially ejected away from the wall and coast directly to the anode with zero secondary wall impacts ($N_{\text{hops}} \to 1$).

## 3. Falsification statement

A $10.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ diameter, $5.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ tall monolithic cylindrical vacuum spacer composed of alternating coaxial dielectric discs of fused silica ($\mathrm{SiO_2}$, $\epsilon_1 = 3.8$, thickness $4.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}$) and rutile titania ($\mathrm{TiO_2}$, $\epsilon_2 = 100.0$, thickness $16.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, total periodic pitch $\Lambda = 20.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, $250$ periods), smooth-polished on the outer vacuum wall ($R_a < 15\ \mathrm{nm}$) and tested in $10^{-5}\ \mathrm{Pa}$ vacuum under both positive and negative DC high voltage:
1. Conditioned surface flashover holdoff must reach $E_{\text{flash}} \ge 35.0\ \mathrm{MV/m}$ in **both** voltage polarities ($\pm 175\ \mathrm{kV}$ across $5.0\ \mathrm{mm}$), outperforming a smooth monolithic $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ control ($6\ \mathrm{MV/m}$) and a smooth monolithic $\mathrm{TiO_2}$ control ($4\ \mathrm{MV/m}$) by $> 5\times$;
2. Witness-plate / Faraday-cup current mapping positioned $2.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ radially outside the insulator wall must detect radial electron ejection current during pre-breakdown conditioning, confirming electrons are steered off the wall into vacuum rather than hopping along the surface.

The hypothesis is killed if flashover occurs below $E_{\text{flash}} < 12.0\ \mathrm{MV/m}$ in either polarity, or if the alternating stack underperforms a homogeneous $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ spacer of identical surface roughness.

## 4. Hypothesis

In a periodic stack of contrasting permittivities ($\epsilon_2 / \epsilon_1 \approx 26.3$), displacement continuity ($D_z = \epsilon(z) E_z$) concentrates the tangential electric field inside the low-permittivity $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ layers ($E_z$ modulation $\Delta E \approx 0.85\, E_0$). By Laplace's equation in vacuum ($\nabla^2 \phi = 0$), this spatial harmonic decays exponentially away from the wall as $\phi_1(x,z) = (\Delta E / k)\sin(kz)e^{-kx}$ ($k = 2\pi/\Lambda = 3.14\times 10^5\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$). An electron moving along $z$ experiences an effective alternating-gradient oscillation at $\omega_{\text{eff}} = k v_z$, generating a time-averaged **radial ponderomotive potential barrier** $U_{\text{pond}}(x) \propto e^{-2kx}$ that peaks at the wall ($x = 0$). For $E_0 = 30\ \mathrm{MV/m}$ and $E_k = 20\ \mathrm{eV}$, this creates an outward radial repelling field $E_{\text{pond},x}(0) \approx 25.9\ \mathrm{MV/m}$, more than $8\times$ stronger than the $3.0\ \mathrm{MV/m}$ surface-charge attraction, ejecting electrons radially into the vacuum gap so they coast directly to the anode without hitting the insulator a second time, independent of voltage polarity.

## 5. Why convention is insufficient here

1. **Angled conical insulators ($45^\circ$):** Steer electrons away from the wall in one polarity, but slam electrons *into* the wall in the opposite polarity, failing under AC or bipolar pulsed stress.
2. **External magnetic insulation ($\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$):** Requires heavy external electromagnet coils ($B > 1\ \mathrm{T}$) and continuous power, and cannot be integrated into compact HV vacuum feedthroughs.
3. **Surface micro-grooves / coatings:** Leave the vacuum trajectory curved toward the wall, relying solely on wall geometry or material $\delta_{\text{SEE}}$ to survive repeated impacts.

Convention fails because it treats the vacuum electrostatic field as uniform along $z$, overlooking the polarity-independent outward ponderomotive force generated by a static periodic $\nabla |\mathbf{E}_1|^2$ gradient.

### Step 1: Periodic Vacuum Field Harmonic

Because $\epsilon_2 \gg \epsilon_1$, axial electric field concentrates in the $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ bands, producing a surface potential harmonic $\phi_1(0,z) = (\Delta E / k)\sin(kz)$. Solving $\nabla^2 \phi_1 = 0$ for $x > 0$ yields $\phi_1(x,z) = (\Delta E / k)\sin(kz)e^{-kx}$, so $|\mathbf{E}_1(x)|^2 = \Delta E^2 e^{-2kx}$.

### Step 2: Bipolar Radial Trajectory Ejection

An electron moving along $z$ with kinetic energy $E_k = \frac{1}{2}m_e v_z^2$ averages over the spatial modulation at $\omega_{\text{eff}} = k v_z$, experiencing a strictly outward radial force $F_x = -\partial U_{\text{pond}}/\partial x > 0$ that scales as $(\Delta E)^2$ (invariant under $V \to -V$). Once pushed past $x > \Lambda / (2\pi) = 3.18\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, the electron clears the surface-charge boundary layer and travels ballistically to the anode.

## 7. Mathematical model

1. **Ponderomotive Potential Barrier:**
   $$U_{\text{pond}}(x) = \frac{e^2 \Delta E^2}{4 m_e (k v_z)^2} e^{-2 k x} = \frac{(e \Delta E / k)^2}{8 E_k} e^{-2 k x}$$
   Dimensional check: $([\mathrm{C}][\mathrm{V/m}]/[\mathrm{m^{-1}}])^2 / [\mathrm{J}] = [\mathrm{J}^2]/[\mathrm{J}] = \mathrm{J}$ (or $\mathrm{eV}$). Valid.

2. **Effective Outward Radial Field at $x = 0$:**
   $$E_{\text{pond},x}(0) = -\frac{1}{e}\frac{\partial U_{\text{pond}}}{\partial x}\Big|_{x=0} = \frac{e \Delta E^2}{4 k E_k}$$
   Dimensional check: $[\mathrm{C}][\mathrm{V^2/m^2}] / ([\mathrm{m^{-1}}][\mathrm{J}]) = \mathrm{V/m}$. Valid.
   For $\Delta E = 25.5\ \mathrm{MV/m}$, $k = 3.142\times 10^5\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, $E_k = 20\ \mathrm{eV}$:
   $$U_{\text{pond}}(0) = \frac{(81.2\ \mathrm{eV})^2}{160\ \mathrm{eV}} = 41.2\ \mathrm{eV}, \qquad E_{\text{pond},x}(0) = 25.9\ \mathrm{MV/m} \gg |E_{\text{charge},x}|$$

3. **Ejection Condition:**
   $$E_{\text{pond},x}(0) > |E_{\text{charge},x}| \implies E_k < \frac{e \Delta E^2}{4 k |E_{\text{charge},x}|}$$
   For $|E_{\text{charge},x}| = 3.0\ \mathrm{MV/m}$, trajectory ejection holds for all electrons up to $E_k = 172.6\ \mathrm{eV}$.

## 8. Physical constraints

1. **Adiabatic Ponderomotive Regime:** The averaged force model holds when the electron traverses multiple periods ($\Lambda = 20\ \mu\mathrm{m}$) before colliding; electrons emitted with zero $v_z$ inside a $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ peak gain $E_k$ over the first $5\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ before entering the periodic regime.
2. **Interfacial Dielectric Breakdown:** High field concentration inside the $4\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ bands ($E_{\text{local}} \approx 4.5\, E_0$) limits macroscopic $E_0$ to $\sim 50\ \mathrm{MV/m}$ before bulk $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ breakdown ($> 200\ \mathrm{MV/m}$).

## 9. Contradiction ledger

1. **First-Half-Period Non-Adiabatic Capture:** An electron emitted at $v_z \approx 0$ directly inside a high-field $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ band may be pulled back to the wall within the first $2\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ before experiencing one full $\Lambda$ period. Requires 3D PIC trajectory simulation to quantify first-hop escape fraction.
2. **Triple-Junction Metal-Dielectric Interface:** If the cathode rests directly on a low-$\epsilon$ $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ band, field enhancement accelerates emission; cathode termination must sit on a high-$\epsilon$ $\mathrm{TiO_2}$ guard layer.

## 10. Experiment

* **Test Article:** $10.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ diameter $\times 5.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ tall sintered/co-fired $\mathrm{SiO_2}/\mathrm{TiO_2}$ alternating-disc cylinder ($\Lambda = 20\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, $250$ periods, mirror-polished outer wall $R_a < 15\ \mathrm{nm}$).
* **Metrology:** Bipolar $\pm 250\ \mathrm{kV}$ DC HV supply in $10^{-5}\ \mathrm{Pa}$ vacuum, picoammeter, and a radially offset Faraday cup ($2.0\ \mathrm{mm}$ outside wall) to measure ejected electron flux.
* **Controls:** Smooth monolithic $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ cylinder and smooth monolithic $\mathrm{TiO_2}$ cylinder of identical geometry.
* **Null Result Definition:** Flashover below $12.0\ \mathrm{MV/m}$ in either polarity, or zero radial electron current detected at the offset Faraday cup prior to breakdown.

## 11. Expected observations

* **Success Branch:** Smooth controls flash over at $4$ to $8\ \mathrm{MV/m}$; alternating $\mathrm{SiO_2}/\mathrm{TiO_2}$ stack holds $\ge 35\ \mathrm{MV/m}$ in both polarities while the offset Faraday cup records radial ejected electrons.
* **Failure Branch:** First-hop capture inside $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ bands or interfacial bulk breakdown triggers flashover at $6$ to $10\ \mathrm{MV/m}$.
