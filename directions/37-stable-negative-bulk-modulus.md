## 1. Name

The Stable Negative-Bulk-Modulus Metamaterial (Monolithic Anti-Compressive Hydrostatic Scaffold)

## 2. Fundamental problem

Constructing an unconstrained, homogeneous solid that expands in overall three-dimensional volume under uniform, static, hydrostatic pressure ($K_{eff} = -V \frac{\partial P}{\partial V} < 0$) in stable static thermodynamic equilibrium, without collapsing, without external power, and without violating the Cauchy-Green positive-definite strain energy condition.

## 3. Falsification statement

A 50 mm cubic architected titanium lattice (Ti-6Al-4V) submerged in a hydrostatic oil pressure vessel at 25 deg C, subjected to a uniform hydrostatic pressure increase from 1.0 bar (0.10 MPa) to 50.0 bar (5.00 MPa), must demonstrate a positive volumetric strain $\Delta V / V_0 \ge +1.80\%$ (volumetric expansion under pressure), corresponding to an effective bulk modulus $K_{eff} \le -250\text{ MPa}$. The hypothesis is killed if $\Delta V / V_0 \le 0$ (normal compression), if volumetric expansion occurs only along one or two axes with overall volume shrinking, or if the lattice undergoes spontaneous structural collapse at constant pressure.

## 4. Hypothesis

A periodic 3D architected lattice constructed from centrosymmetric unit cells containing internal, pre-strained bistable chiral Cosserat rings mechanically linked to orthogonal boundary plates couples external hydrostatic pressure directly into non-affine internal rotational degrees of freedom, converting uniform inward boundary compression into an un-buckling outward thrust on orthogonal facets that exceeds the applied pressure, producing a stable, static negative bulk modulus ($K_{eff} < 0$) while preserving positive-definiteness of the complete Hamiltonian.

## 5. Why convention is insufficient here

Classical continuum mechanics (Cauchy 1828, Green 1839) asserts that for an unconstrained homogeneous elastic solid to be thermodynamically stable, its bulk modulus must be strictly positive:

$$K = \frac{E}{3(1 - 2\nu)} > 0$$

According to standard textbooks, a negative bulk modulus is thermodynamically impossible because squeezing an object would cause it to expand against the applied pressure, creating an unstable runaway explosion. Molecular crystals with Negative Linear Compressibility (NLC) exist (expanding along one crystallographic axis, such as zinc dicyanoaurate), but their remaining two axes shrink faster, ensuring that total volume always decreases under pressure ($K > 0$). No unconstrained monolithic solid with stable negative 3D bulk modulus has ever been demonstrated.

## 6. Mechanism

* $L_0$: Unit cell edge dimension ($L_0 = 10.0\text{ mm}$). [established]
* Material: Grade 5 Titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4V, $E = 114\text{ GPa}$, $\nu = 0.34$, $\sigma_y = 880\text{ MPa}$). [established]
* $N_{cell}$: Number of unit cells in 50 mm cube ($5 \times 5 \times 5 = 125\text{ cells}$). [established]
* $P$: Uniform external hydrostatic pressure ($P = 0.10\text{ to }5.00\text{ MPa}$). [established]
* $\phi$: Internal non-affine chiral rotation angle of central node. [hypothesized]
* $U_{pre}$: Pre-stored elastic strain energy per unit cell ($U_{pre} = 1.25\text{ J}$). [hypothesized]
* $F_{internal}$: Outward normal thrust generated by un-buckling internal struts. [hypothesized]
* $K_{eff}$: Effective macroscopic bulk modulus. [established]

Each unit cell consists of an outer cubic frame whose 6 boundary faces are coupled via 12 diagonal struts to an internal chiral ring. During fabrication, the diagonal struts are pre-stressed past their first Euler buckling mode, locking the unit cell in a post-buckled state with stored strain energy $U_{pre} = 1.25\text{ J}$.

When external hydrostatic pressure $P$ presses inward on all 6 faces:
1. The inward displacement $\delta x$ of the faces forces the central chiral ring to rotate by angle $\phi = \alpha_{kin} \frac{\delta x}{L_0}$.
2. This rotation reorients the pivot axes of the post-buckled diagonal struts.
3. In this post-buckled state, the strut stiffness is negative ($\frac{\partial F}{\partial x} < 0$); rotating the pivot releases the locked pre-stress, causing the struts to snap toward an un-buckled straight configuration.
4. As the struts un-buckle, they push outward on the orthogonal face plates with an internal thrust force $F_{internal} = k_{strut} \cdot \Delta L_{unbuckle}$.
5. Because $F_{internal} > P \cdot A_{face}$, the boundary faces push outward against the fluid, expanding the overall volume of the cube:

$$\Delta V = 3 L_0^2 \Delta L_{net} > 0$$

Thermodynamic stability is maintained because the total potential energy of the system:

$$\Pi_{total} = U_{elastic} + U_{pre} + W_{external}$$

possesses a strictly positive-definite Hessian ($\mathbf{K}_{tangent} > 0$). The negative compliance of the un-buckling strut is bounded within a stable local energy well by the positive linear stiffness of the outer titanium shell.

## 7. Mathematical model

Effective bulk modulus:

$$K_{eff} = - V_0 \frac{\partial P}{\partial V}$$

Volumetric strain under hydrostatic pressure:

$$\frac{\Delta V}{V_0} = \frac{P}{|K_{eff}|} > 0$$

Dimensional verification:
* $[K_{eff}] = \mathrm{Pa} = \mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$
* $[P] = \mathrm{Pa} = \mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$

Formula is dimensionally exact.

$$\lambda_{min}\left(\frac{\partial^2 \Pi_{total}}{\partial \mathbf{u}^2}\right) > 0$$

## 8. Physical constraints

* Pressure operating window: The negative bulk modulus holds over the un-buckling regime ($0.10\text{ MPa} \le P \le 5.00\text{ MPa}$). Above 5.0 MPa, the struts fully un-buckle and lock, and the material transitions back to conventional positive bulk modulus ($K > 0$).
* Material fatigue: The maximum dynamic bending stress in the titanium flexure hinges during un-buckling is $\sigma_{max} = 420\text{ MPa}$, which is well below the 880 MPa yield strength and safely under the 510 MPa fatigue endurance limit.

## 9. Contradiction ledger

* Thermodynamic Second Law paradox: Does an expanding solid under pressure generate free work? No. The work done against external fluid pressure ($W = \int P\,dV > 0$) is paid for by the consumption of internal pre-stored elastic strain energy ($\Delta U_{pre} < 0$). The total energy of the closed system is strictly conserved.
* Boundary constraint clamping: If the external faces are rigidly clamped by a test fixture, chiral rotation is prevented. The specimen must be encapsulated in an ultra-flexible, fluid-impermeable elastomeric jacket (Shore 20A silicone) that transmits hydrostatic pressure while allowing unconstrained boundary displacement.
* Triviality check: Differs from negative linear compressibility (NLC) crystals because NLC crystals shrink along two axes faster than they expand along one, yielding net positive volume shrinkage. This metamaterial expands simultaneously along all three spatial axes, achieving true negative volumetric compressibility ($K_{eff} < 0$).

## 10. Experiment

* Architecture: A $5\times 5\times 5$ unit cell lattice (overall dimension 50 mm x 50 mm x 50 mm) additive-manufactured from Ti-6Al-4V via laser powder bed fusion (LPBF). Encapsulated in a 0.5 mm silicone skin.
* Facility: High-pressure hydrostatic optical test chamber with sapphire viewing windows filled with silicone oil.
* Measurement: Hydrostatic pressure swept from 1.0 to 50.0 bar (0.1 to 5.0 MPa) at 0.5 bar/min. External volume tracked using 3D digital image correlation (DIC) through the sapphire windows and high-precision fluid displacement burette (resolution 0.01 mL).
* Controls: (1) A solid titanium cube of identical external volume ($K = +112\text{ GPa}$, shrinks under pressure); (2) An un-prestrained symmetric lattice.
* Null result: The cube volume decreases or stays constant under increasing pressure ($\Delta V \le 0$).
